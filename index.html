A car loses this race

At the worst hour of the day, BUET measured surface traffic in Dhaka at 4.8 kilometers an hour. A comfortable walking pace is about five. Above the same street, the elevated Metro Rail runs at 35.

One kilometer, three ways, running at once. All three markers share a single track and cover it in exact proportion to their published speeds, so the gap you see between the car and the walker is the real one: about thirty seconds over a kilometer, in the walker's favor. The clock is compressed — the walker takes twelve minutes in life and eight seconds here — but nothing else is. The race restarts itself.

How it got this slow

Different research teams have measured Dhaka at different moments and in different ways. Put every published figure on one axis and the line only travels in one direction. Hover a point, or tab through them, to see who measured it and what they measured.

A city-wide average and a peak-hour average measure different things, so each point carries its own label. The 2035 figure is a projection, not an observation.

The roads never grew

Every other chart on this page is a symptom. This is the cause. Over the decade to 2005, the traffic on Dhaka's roads grew twenty-seven times faster than the roads carrying it.

The city breathes twice a day

Every spoke is one hour. The longer and greener it reaches, the faster the roads are running. Dhaka falls apart before 9:00 AM, half recovers over lunch, then sinks again and stays there until well past 10:00 PM.

The gridlock has a spine

Those hours priced at a Tk 45,000 monthly salary

This calculation models one kind of commuter: someone with a fixed workplace, a five-day week, and a salary to price the lost hours against. It is not most of Dhaka. The people who lose most to this traffic are the ones who cannot buy their way around it — who walk because the fare is the choice between transport and something else, or who ride standing in a bus for two hours each way on a wage no calculator here is set up to value. Their cost does not appear in the grid above.

Between midnight and 5:00 AM, it is a different city

For five hours a night the roads carry only what they were built to carry, and Dhaka moves at 25 kilometers an hour. Then the sun comes up.
